Approved Greenlands loot models: eight exact source GLBs and hashed principal-span measurements live in `GameClient/ArtSource/LootItems/Approved/`. `tools/Import-LootItems.py` imports them through per-item Interchange pipelines under `/Game/Frontier/Gameplay/LootItems/`, uniformly fits measured end-to-end length to `content/items/forge-handoff.json`, keeps the Nanite fallback at 100% of source triangles for `-game` rendering, preserves authored wolf-pelt normals while recomputing its tangents, and sets `USFItemDefinition.WorldMesh`; `tools/Check-LootImport.py` validates those persisted links, fallback meshes and pelt build settings. `tools/Build-LootDisplay.py` builds the development-only `SB_LootModels` sandbox with rest poses, a 10 cm cube per item and an actor-mirrored instance of the single bracer; `tools/Capture-LootDisplay.py` takes 1920×1080 `-game` captures, `tools/Capture-LootDetails.py` adds close/unlit diagnostic views, and `tools/Make-LootRound3.py` pairs captures with frozen references. Motion jobs (`kind: "motion"`, `MOTION_PARAMS`/`run_motion`, or Animate on a `_rigged` model) animate a rigged model: `motion/render_views.py` renders its rest pose front | left side in Blender, the host's local quantized MiniMax H3 (`motion_graph`, model files in `H3`, optional turbo LoRA) films the action from that first frame. Each rigged model gets a joint detector (`train_detector`, run by a `detector` job, queued on rigging while Settings `detector_auto_train` is on, or by a motion job that finds none; cached in `Generated/.detectors/<model hash>[-t<turn>]-v<DETECTOR_VERSION>`, so bumping the version retrains every model, and a retrain keeps the better of old and new by exam p95): `motion/synth_render.py` renders random poses within joint ranges read from the skeleton (`joint_ranges`) from the same two cameras, `motion/detector.py` fine-tunes DINOv2 (ViT-S/14, or ViT-B/14), warm-started from the best good detector of the same skeleton category (`synth_render.category`, `warm_source`), to find each main joint in both views (left and right apart, which a point tracker can't do in a side view), and `motion/exam.py` grades the baked result of an unseen motion at 32–80 % of each joint's range (`motion/selftest_render.py`, which also writes the joints neither camera sees; `grade` reports those apart) filmed like a real video into a band (`detector_band`: good / usable / failed against the Settings `detector_limits` and `detector_usable_x`). `GET /api/detector`, the `detector` block in `/api/ls` and on jobs expose it. Then `motion/track_solve.py --detector` finds the joints in each take (whatever the band: it is a label, not a gate), rebuilds them in 3D and scores the take by front/side height agreement, cuts a looping action (`LOOPING`, `--loop`) to its best loop with the seam blended (`loop_cut`, `close_loop`), and `motion/bake.py` aims each bone at its next tracked joint (only the hips translate) and exports an animated GLB; `motion/limbs.py` (not wired into jobs yet) paints each limb a fixed left/right colour from the skin weights, renders the same two views and fails a rig whose limbs split, bleed into each other, differ left to right, or whose bones move skin far from themselves (`limbs.json`); `--fix OUT.glb` hands such far-off weight to the bone nearest each vertex and saves the repaired model; SSH hosts only, prepared by `host-setup/install-motion.sh`.
