# Legends of the Shattered Frontier - Building and Territory System Architecture
## Version 1.0

## 1. Purpose and ownership

This defines the Unreal game's claim lifecycle, permissions, settlement government, upkeep, and territorial sieges. [Construction](Legends_Shattered_Frontier_Construction_System_Architecture_v0.1.md) owns placement, terrain editing, support, comfort, material behavior, and automation. [Game Master](Legends_Shattered_Frontier_Game_Master_Architecture_v0.1.md) owns GM identity and cross-domain powers. [Tuning and Configuration](Legends_Shattered_Frontier_Tuning_and_Configuration_Architecture_v0.1.md) owns publishing and operating controls. These are intended design contracts, not delivered runtime features.

The objective is expressive, persistent homes with useful survival functions and consequential, recoverable territorial conflict. Our synthesis uses Rust's visible building authority and maintenance store as a reference; its automatic decay-to-destruction is not our abandonment policy. Facepunch documents privilege visualization, resource upkeep and maintenance in its [official Tool Cupboard guide](https://wiki.facepunch.com/rust/the_tool_cupboard) and [Devblog 189](https://rust.facepunch.com/news/devblog-189). The specific protections, costs, progression thresholds and siege schedule below are Shattered Frontier design choices, not claims about those games.

## 2. Claims and land ownership

### 2.1 Types and establishment

| Claim type | Launch entitlement and volume | Conflict policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal plot | One per account per world; 40 m × 40 m × 30 m | Home protection; no hostile structure damage or capture |
| Guild claim | One settlement per guild per world; initial 160 m × 160 m × 60 m | Only frontier regions permit declared territorial sieges |
| City district | Partition within a settlement, with boundaries selected on the claim map | Inherits parent policy; designated residential parcels retain home protection |
| Strategic territory | Authored world objective with an explicit capture perimeter | Always uses declared territorial siege rules |

A claim anchor is a crafted placeable costing 20 rough timber and 10 granite, matching Crafting's launch catalogue. Crafting owns those item definitions; these quantities are recipe content defaults. Placement previews the complete volume, protected routes, hazards, upkeep, conflict policy, and adjacent claims. The claim is created only when permission, entitlement, stock and exclusive land reservation commit together. Anchors cannot reserve uneditable landmarks, resource nodes, watercourses or critical routes, nor block designated public access corridors. Exclusions are visible before confirmation. A claim cannot straddle zone authority boundaries at launch.

Claims may touch but never overlap unrelated ownership; a 5 m clear buffer separates unrelated new claim boundaries, excluding public corridors whose authored width takes precedence. First successful authoritative reservation wins concurrent attempts. A denied reservation consumes nothing. Terrain excavations and support anchors must remain inside the permitted volume. Claim boundaries never authorize tunneling into another claim or undermine its supports.

Owners may reposition an anchor inside their existing claim without changing the boundary. Territory expansion uses the map and settlement upgrade rules, checks the entire added volume, and takes effect atomically. Expansion cannot engulf another claim, bypass a corridor or cause an existing build to become illegal. Shrinking is refused while affected structures, containers or dependent support paths remain outside the proposed boundary; the preview identifies what must move. Unpaid or besieged claims cannot expand, shrink, merge or move boundaries.

Same-owner adjacent parcels can be grouped for management; their resource, siege, district and supply boundaries persist. Joining is not an entitlement or build-volume multiplier. Cross-claim Builder's Cache sourcing is not supported at launch. Guild alliances confer no implicit land or storage rights. A personal owner may transfer their plot by recipient acceptance when the recipient has a free entitlement; guild land transfers use governance and a receiving guild's free settlement entitlement. The transaction preserves contents' provenance and logs both authorities. Transfers cannot remove siege notice, cooldown or debt.

### 2.2 Lifecycle and abandonment

A funded claim is active. When its prepaid reserve expires it enters seven days of grace, retaining protections and services. After grace it becomes delinquent: new construction, expansion, upgrades and automated production stop; repair, maintenance deposits, manual storage retrieval and demolition remain available. Existing shelter, comfort and authorized entry remain usable. Neglect reduces condition under Construction's rules, with a floor of 1.

Thirty consecutive unpaid days trigger a final seven-day reclamation notice. Payment before reclamation cancels that notice. At its end the server durably archives the complete terrain/construction delta, ownership, contents and blueprint before releasing the land. Items are transferred to an owner-only recovery store without duplication; the old world entities cease to exist in the same committed operation. Unresolved archive or inventory transfers block release and alert operators. Personal recovery is free, remains assigned to the last lawful individual owner and is retained until account deletion; rebuilding requires a free legal parcel and materials already recovered. Guild-owned recovery remains governed by guild authority while the guild exists. On dissolution, its frozen recovery stock follows Backend's 30-day guild-bank retention and final destruction; individually owned housing and unspent lots still legally owned by an individual (excluding donated/cache-controller-owned stock) are segregated to their last lawful personal owners first, with ledger receipts preventing duplicate guild/personal recovery. Guild membership alone never grants a member the entire recovery pool. Archived structures do not restore on top of a new owner. World terrain returns to the regional baseline only after checking neighboring support and protected routes; unsafe release waits for an operator's safe restoration plan.

Voluntary abandonment uses the same archive/recovery process after a 72-hour cancellation period. It cannot begin during a declared siege, and a pending siege pauses reclamation until resolved. Guild disbanding starts this process and not immediate deletion. Anchor destruction during a siege cannot bypass claim capture. Logging in does not reset unpaid time; an upkeep payment does. Notifications appear at reserve exhaustion, grace end, and reclamation notice in the claim panel and account notification inbox; no external messaging integration is assumed.

## 3. Permissions and storage

Permissions are named actions. Presets grant individual rights; being a friend, ally or guild member alone grants none. Explicit actor restrictions override role grants. Region protections and siege rules remain authoritative. The claim panel offers Visitor, Resident, Builder, Steward and Governor presets with expandable individual switches and an effective-permission preview.

| Action | Visitor | Resident | Builder | Steward | Governor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enter unlocked/public areas; use designated public services | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Use assigned doors, beds and resident facilities | No | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Repair and place structures | No | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Shape terrain | No | No | Explicit grant | Yes | Yes |
| Demolish structures | No | No | Own placements only | Yes | Yes |
| Configure automation and defenses | No | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Deposit maintenance materials | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Manage claim maintenance reserve | No | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Assign Builder or lower roles; manage districts | No | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Appoint Stewards, change taxes, expand/transfer/abandon land | No | No | No | No | Yes |
| Open/deposit/withdraw ordinary containers | Container grant | Container grant | Container grant | Container grant | Container grant |
| Draw automatically from Builder's Cache | No | No | If enabled | If enabled | If enabled |

The personal owner initially has Governor rights; a guild governor is appointed by guild leadership. Public initially has Visitor rights. Newly invited residents and guild members receive only Visitor until assigned. No one may delegate rights they do not hold or remove the final legitimate governor. Door access and workstation operation are distinct object grants. Private residential containers stay private even to civic governors; authorized support recovery requires the GM process. Public storage withdrawal is a separate deliberate switch with a warning and preview.

Automatic construction supply defaults off per Builder's Cache. Enabling it requires cache-owner consent and the claim's supply permission; it permits only eligible build/repair consumption, not browsing or manual withdrawal. Cache restrictions may narrow but never expand claim rights. Construction's same-claim, 20 m, eight-cache and atomic-consumption rules apply. Siege lockdown, loss of a role, revoked supply, destroyed storage or transfer invalidates previews and takes effect at the next transaction. Unused materials never become a builder's property through a canceled plan.

## 4. Upkeep and condition

The claim anchor has a separate maintenance reserve, visibly distinct from Builder's Caches and personal storage. Automatic payment uses only this reserve; any player can donate, while only Stewards/Governors may withdraw uncommitted supplies. The panel shows today's charge by material, days funded, and the effect of a proposed build. Prepayment is capped at 30 days at current cost. Required stock is reserved in hourly atomic charges; cost fractions accumulate and round only at debit so splitting pieces cannot reduce maintenance.

Launch upkeep is 1% of the recoverable construction recipe materials per real day for structural pieces and functional objects, excluding decorative furnishings, personal recovery stores and protected authored civic objects. Empty land has no material upkeep but still requires the anchor's minimum daily 1 worked timber and 1 stone. The minimum applies instead of any lower assessed amount while upkeep is enabled. A published 0% upkeep setting disables all charges including this minimum, prospectively reactivates unpaid claims and cancels their pending unpaid reclamation; it does not reverse completed archives or voluntary abandonment. Quantity is based on authored replacement costs, never current market prices or trade history; damage does not lower it. The reserve pays each required material separately; shortage starts grace without taking a partial hourly payment. There is no accumulated debt while delinquent: paying the next full daily cost reactivates the claim after current permission/siege checks.

Upkeep prevents neglect wear; it does not stop weather exposure, repair siege damage or grant stronger structural support. After grace, neglect removes 1 condition per elapsed real day, prorated per simulation interval and multiplied by the approved neglect-wear scale (100% default, 0–200% in 5% steps). Environmental and neglect wear cannot destroy pieces or indirectly trigger structural collapse by reducing support capacity. Shelter deterioration may interrupt comfort or devices, with a visible cause; combat damage and deliberate support removal retain Construction's collapse rules. A condition of 1 preserves the damaged structure for repair. Land reclamation is the explicit archival process above, not silent decay-to-deletion.

## 5. Settlement progression and government

Upgrades are earned through useful buildings and sustained participation, not decorative voxel count. A qualifying resident must explicitly register their home and have completed a non-idle gameplay action in the last 14 days; each account counts once. Facilities must be complete, reachable and operational. Personal residential parcels consume the parent build budget rather than multiplying it.

| Stage | Requirements (including all prior stage facilities) | Maximum connected settlement volume | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Camp | Guild anchor, 3 registered residents, shelter, maintenance reserve funded for 3 days | 160 m × 160 m × 60 m | Shared workshop, storage, district assignments |
| Village | 5 residents; 5 sheltered beds; operational sawmill and water supply; requirements held 3 days | 200 m × 200 m × 60 m | Community services, resident home parcels |
| Town | 10 residents; 10 beds; forge, infirmary and food production; held 7 days | 240 m × 240 m × 80 m | Market services, council, public transport destination |
| City | 20 residents; 20 beds; staffed civic center, two production chains and defenses; held 14 days | 320 m × 320 m × 100 m | Multiple civic districts and advanced automation functions; the total 256-device claim cap does not increase |
| Capital | 40 residents; 40 beds; City services and a controlled strategic territory; held 21 days | 400 m × 400 m × 120 m | Regional diplomatic seat, capital title and regional event participation |

Upgrade uses Governor confirmation of a full cost/land/upkeep preview; it consumes no arbitrary currency fee beyond construction of required facilities. Additional land must be available; an upgrade grants an entitlement ceiling, not automatic neighboring land. Rebuilding or temporary resident absence never deletes an earned stage. Missing operating requirements suspend the corresponding service after a seven-day grace; restoring requirements reactivates it. Capital strategic-territory loss immediately suspends its territorial benefits and title use until restored, while buildings persist.

Guild leadership appoints one mayor/governor and up to five councillors. Councillors can propose policies and act as Stewards; majority council approval plus Governor confirmation is required for treasury withdrawals exceeding one day of upkeep, territorial transfer and voluntary abandonment. With no council, guild leader confirmation supplies the second authority. A single-person guild may maintain Camp but cannot unlock council-dependent stages until it has the qualifying residents. Replacing inactive officers uses guild leadership's recorded succession rules, never container possession. Every government change is recorded and visible to members.

Town and higher settlements may charge 0–5% service tax on explicitly taxable public market services, displayed before acceptance and collected through Economy's ledger. Tax never takes resources directly from inventories, Builder's Caches, trade not using the service, or upkeep donations. Increases require 24-hour posted notice. Strategic territory supplies a launch 5% local gathering yield benefit to its controlling guild, without stacking across territories or affecting protected resource nodes. Transport requires unlocked destination access and stops during siege; it cannot evacuate contested stock mid-fight.

## 6. Declared territorial sieges

### 6.1 Eligibility, declaration and scheduling

Only frontier guild claims and strategic territories are capturable. Protected personal homes, starter areas, designated residential parcels, their storage and critical civic infrastructure remain immune to hostile structural damage and capture. Each eligible claim displays its contested assets and protected parcels before players build. Protected areas cannot host offensive defenses, siege equipment, shared wartime stock or the control point; moving objects into protection during notice or battle is refused. World event threats follow their announced domain rules and cannot silently authorize PvP damage.

An attacking guild needs an active claim, five participating accounts and the target in a frontier region. A guild can have one outgoing siege; a target can have only one incoming siege. Allies join a declared side before the battle, and cannot damage protected bystanders. Declaration consumes a bond of 200 stone and 100 worked timber; half is returned only if the attack is canceled within one hour, with a 24-hour retry lock. After that the bond is a committed sink. Material item definitions belong to Crafting.

The defender publishes a recurring three-hour daily availability window in world server time, shown in the viewer's local time. Declaration guarantees at least 24 hours' notice; the first published window starting in the following 24 hours is selected and locked. Changing availability requires seven days' notice and cannot alter pending battles. New eligible claims get seven days of initial protection and must choose a window before that protection expires; the region's advertised default window applies otherwise. No offline defender vote or online-presence prerequisite can postpone a valid battle. Scheduled downtime reschedules the complete window with renewed 24-hour notice, never shortens it.

### 6.2 Battle and outcome

The battle lasts three hours. Attackers may breach contested walls, doors, defenses, automation and shared storage using authorized siege damage. They cannot freely terraform, tunnel under protected areas or replace the ownership anchor. Defender repairs pay Construction's published proportional quote for health actually restored and obey §6.3's incremental combat-repair restrictions, support rules and interrupted interactions. Declared sides cannot transfer contested assets out of claim storage, pack away the objective, change protection designations or abandon land during notice/battle; ordinary personal inventories remain personal. Shared cache and treasury spending remains available for local repairs, ammunition and defense, with ledgered sinks rather than remote transfers.

Attackers capture the exposed control point by holding it uninterrupted for ten minutes. Any living defending participant within its marked 10 m radius interrupts and resets the hold; attackers must have a living registered participant inside it. The point must have a navigable approach validated before battle. If it becomes unreachable through a faulty authored state, capture pauses and operators restore access; neither side receives an automatic victory. At the three-hour deadline a completed hold wins; otherwise defenders win. A hold cannot extend past the deadline.

Victory transfers only the contested claim authority, military/shared structures, automation and explicitly contested shared stock remaining in the world. Personal inventories, protected residential property and recovery stores do not transfer. Defeated residents retain their protected parcels and retrieval access; the winner controls civic routes subject to public-access rules and may offer new residency terms with seven days' notice. There is no forced confiscation of personal property through tax or government changes. Destroyed eligible pieces create 25% structural-material salvage eligible for either declared side through an explicit, audited siege-loot grant. A successful loot claim transfers only that shell-salvage entitlement once; proximity, construction supply permission or causing destruction grants no access to stored contents or protected recovery. Siege loot is the explicit exception to the ordinary outbound contested-stock restriction; local repair and ammunition use remain the other permitted sinks. Destruction of a container moves its contents once into persistent recovery under their existing controller entitlement, and only capture of eligible contested stock or an explicit authorized loot grant can transfer that entitlement. Damage persists; no automatic full rebuild occurs.

Either outcome gives the target a seven-day siege cooldown, applying to every attacker and surviving transfer. Attack cancellation after its one-hour refund period also locks the target for 24 hours to prevent declaration spam; repeated collusive cancellations are audited. Defenses stop firing on former opponents when the window ends. Existing PvP combat may resolve under Combat's rules, while structure damage immediately returns to the claim policy. All announced costs and durations are frozen to the declaration's configuration revision.

### 6.3 Launch siege equipment, damage and repair pressure

Siege equipment is a placeable functional object registered to one declared side. It deploys only inside the announced battle staging footprint, on navigable contested ground, at least 20 m from the objective and outside protected access corridors. Placement takes an uninterrupted 10 seconds; movement, incoming damage, a changed boundary or invalid support cancels without creating the object. At launch each side may deploy two rams, two ballistae and one mortar per battle. Equipment cannot enter protected parcels, fire through their boundaries or use them as shields. Placement, ammunition and operation require claim-side siege-operation permission and a registered participant. A registered attacker's temporary siege-placement authorization covers only these objects in the staging footprint; it grants no ordinary construction, terrain or storage rights on the enemy claim. Deployed equipment remains vulnerable during the window and is recovered as an inventory item only after battle, if intact and empty; no reclaim refunds spent ammunition or consumed charges.

| Equipment | Launch recipe | Operation and ammunition | Structural damage before material/fire resistance | Vulnerability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Battering ram | 40 worked timber, 10 iron ingots | Two living operators within 3 m; 4-second committed swing; no ammunition; target within 2 m | 80 health to the first contacted structural cell or object | 400 combat health; operators exposed; no movement during a swing |
| Ballista | 30 worked timber, 20 iron ingots | One operator; 8-second reload; one siege bolt per shot (2 worked timber, 1 iron ingot); 60 m maximum range | 120 health to the first solid eligible cell or object hit | 300 combat health; open operator seat; line of sight required |
| Mortar | 20 worked timber, 30 iron ingots | One operator; 12-second reload; one stone charge (10 granite, 1 iron ingot); 20–80 m target range | 100 health to each of at most four exposed cells/objects within 1 m of impact | 250 combat health; fixed firing position; 3-second marked impact warning |

Recipes are authored launch content owned by Crafting and shown before acquisition; their identifiers must be registered there before implementation. Ordinary melee and handheld projectile attacks deal 10% of their validated post-mitigation damage to eligible structure health; they cannot damage protected structures. Handheld combat remains useful against operators, ammunition carriers and equipment. Equipment receives normal validated hostile attack damage, and is repairable only under the combat-repair rule below. The weapon table defines siege damage; character offense stats, critical hits and rapid-fire effects do not multiply equipment damage or shorten reloads. Ammunition is debited once when a valid shot launches; a blocked preview consumes nothing, while a launched miss consumes ammunition. Disconnecting or switching operators does not reset reloads. Stationary siege equipment cannot auto-fire or continue unattended.

Damage uses Construction's cell health and occupied shape mask, rather than chunk health or arbitrary mesh triangles. Maximum combat health is the full 0.5 m cell health scaled by occupied fraction, with a minimum of 25% of full-cell health; this health-floor rule is shipped content, not a live setting. A direct hit damages the first occupied mask intersected by its server projectile trace; thin shapes have their proportional health and cannot redirect damage into an empty cell. Excess direct-hit damage does not pass through to another cell. For mortar splash, sort candidates by distance to impact then cell/object identity, test line of sight to each occupied surface, and damage the first four eligible exposed targets. The damage is flat inside the radius; there is no editable falloff expression. Cover blocks targets behind it. Each target receives damage once, even if multiple mask faces are exposed. No splash, penetration, collapse or fire may cross a protected-property boundary or damage the mandatory protected access spine. Any collapse that would orphan a protected property's support is refused by the same support-dependency validation used at placement. Stored inventory is moved or dropped once under Inventory rules; destroying its host never duplicates its contents as salvage.

Defenders counter siege pressure through exterior cover, independent support paths, protected internal repair access, ammunition logistics, and attacks on exposed operators and equipment. Ballista shots are obstructed by intact intervening cells; mortar warnings allow repositioning, and its four-cell cap prevents one projectile deleting a large roof. Automated defenses need valid targeting authorization, normal ammunition/power and line of sight; they cannot fire from protected parcels or target nonparticipants. Cutting their contested power path stops them after their existing authored battery reserve is exhausted. Equipment limits count deployed objects, including disconnected owners' objects, so relogging cannot add weapons.

During the active siege, repairing combat health targets one cell or one functional object at a time. Each uninterrupted 2-second operation restores at most 5% of that target's maximum combat health and uses Construction's published proportional repair quote for the amount actually restored. Only one repair operation may reserve a target at once. The target must have received no hostile hit for 10 seconds; any fresh hostile hit, repairer movement/damage, expired permission or failed material validation cancels without a charge. No combat-health repair can rescue a piece during its unstable-collapse countdown. This prevents parallel repair spam and instant restoration under fire. Environmental-condition repairs remain possible but cannot restore combat health through a different tool mode. New replacement construction cannot occupy a cell destroyed by hostile damage for 30 seconds; support and material checks still apply. Painting/upgrading, moving contested objects and packing siege equipment during battle are prohibited. No repair costs ammunition, restores spent charges or repairs multiple targets through a shared batch. All rates are the frozen battle profile, not an operator's live formula.

The launch automated defense is the **powered bolt turret**, crafted at an Engineering Workshop tier 3 from 30 worked timber and 20 iron ingots. It has 400 combat health, draws 10 power units per elapsed real second while armed, stores 20 siege bolts, and consumes one bolt per fired shot. It uses the ballista's siege bolt recipe (2 worked timber and 1 iron ingot per bolt), a 2-second reload, 20 m range and a fixed 120-degree forward cone shown during placement. Each hit deals 15 base physical damage through Combat's normal mitigation pipeline against an authorized opposing participant, or 15 combat-health damage to opposing deployed siege equipment. It never damages terrain, buildings or containers, so its rounds cannot silently become unattended wall-breaking equipment. Character equipment bonuses, haste and critical chance do not change its authored damage or reload. Ammunition withdrawal uses an Inventory transaction; a launched miss spends the bolt, while a failed pre-shot authorization spends nothing.

At most eight turrets may exist per claim; each counts against the total 256 connected-device claim cap and normal functional-object budget. Its 2 m × 2 m foundation footprint needs independent valid support, 2 m clear headroom, and an unobstructed muzzle. It can operate outdoors and in a contested defensive emplacement; weather exposure follows its authored material, and flooding/submersion or lost support disables it. Its footprint, muzzle and foundation must remain outside protected parcels, protected access corridors, mandatory public/approach clearances and objective gate sockets. Its firing cone may cover contested objective approaches but cannot cross protected parcels or protected access corridors; placing it on an offshore, elevated or otherwise inaccessible support does not waive the reachable-contested-ground and support rules. The preview rejects any firing path using protected housing as cover or crossing protection. Contested walls and friendly bodies physically block shots; turrets never shoot through them.

Stewards/Governors with defense-configuration permission place, orient, arm and set battery priority; ammunition deposit can be separately granted, while withdrawal remains a container permission. Arming displays the siege-only targeting rule; operators cannot type target predicates or assign arbitrary player lists. During an active battle the turret may fire unattended at living registered opposing participants and opposing deployed siege equipment only. It does not fire at allies, residents, bystanders, unregistered players or friendly equipment; there is no friendly fire. A fresh server line-of-sight and target-side check precedes every shot. Nearest authorized visible target is selected, with stable entity-ID tie breaking; a two-second visible lock-on beam/warning precedes the first shot on each new target. Target authorization never follows nameplates or client tags. Leaving range, entering protected property, battle end, power loss, lost permissions or empty ammunition immediately stops firing. Loss of network power permits only an existing battery's authored reserve, not free stored shots. Claim capture disarms surviving turrets and clears target state until the new controller explicitly rearms them. Outside declared battles they remain safe, even while the owner is offline. The three siege machines above require living operators; this dedicated turret is the sole launch unattended combat device.

Turret recipe, health, damage, targeting, warning time, range, ammunition slots, draw and device-count cap are shipped content. Approved future battle profiles may use the existing 50–150% equipment-damage multiplier while preserving its targeting and protection invariants; arbitrary real-time range or target-rule edits are prohibited. Profile changes affect future declarations only and require compatible client presentation.

### 6.4 Protected property, access and capture geometry

A residential parcel is designated only while no siege is announced, and its protection includes the parcel volume, its independently validated support anchors, and a permanent 3 m wide by 3 m high access corridor to the settlement's authored public access spine. The planner displays this envelope. It cannot overlap the objective's approach, staging ground, public spine, another parcel, or a contested military asset. Before assignment, all supporting cells must be inside the protected envelope or immutable terrain; protection cannot depend on a destructible enemy wall. Protection does not spread through touching structures. Parcels cannot encircle an objective, create invulnerable curtain walls or extend offensive coverage; such proposals fail before reservation with the specific blocked corridor highlighted.

The control point has a 10 m capture radius, 4 m clear headroom and two separately navigable 3 m wide by 3 m high approach corridors joining different sides of the outer claim boundary to the radius. The routes are designated when the claim becomes siege-eligible. Contestable doors and gates may stand at displayed gate sockets on these routes; permanent walls, solid fill, furniture, containers and protected property may not obstruct their clear space. Gates remain siege-damageable. The gate sockets and objective cannot be moved after eligibility except through a reviewed relocation with seven-day notice and no incoming siege. Terrain edits cannot destroy the underlying navigable ground. The server rechecks approach geometry, door breakability, support dependencies and route connection after each relevant edit and before battle. Unauthorized changes fail atomically, rather than forcing attackers to request GM intervention.

Combatants receive no safe firing position inside protected property: offensive player attacks, offensive buffs and siege equipment targeting across its boundary are refused; a participant who enters a protected home cannot attack or contribute to capture until leaving. Neither declared side may build, park equipment, stack containers, redirect hostile hazards or lock a gate across the protected access spine. Gate access on that spine is always available to the parcel's lawful occupants. Region travel rules still apply; the spine provides physical access rather than instant teleportation or immunity elsewhere in the siege area.

After capture, protected residents have continuing home and corridor rights during the seven-day residency notice. A resident may accept the new terms and remain, or reject them and relocate: rejection or notice expiry archives the parcel and its private contents into that owner's recovery store before releasing the parcel reservation. The resident is never charged the winner's retroactive tax. Failure to commit recovery leaves the parcel protected and alerts operators. Accepting residents receive the new government policy prospectively; refusing residents cannot leave a permanent protected enclave after safe recovery succeeds. Claim authorities cannot deny corridor access or attack housing to accelerate this process. The winner receives contested assets immediately but can build on a former protected parcel only after its archive/release transaction completes. Objective and permanent public corridors remain excluded from building in every ownership state.

Equipment recipes, health/damage tables, targeting rules, protection geometry and repair algorithm are shipped, versioned content/code with compatible client presentation. Reviewed siege profiles may vary equipment damage from 50–150% of standard in 10% steps and deployed counts within the shipped caps; a profile cannot remove protected-property, mask, line-of-sight, single-target-repair or archive-before-release requirements. Profile changes apply only to future declarations. Combat owns execution and damage attribution, Construction owns cell/object health and support, Crafting owns recipe/ammunition definitions, and Territory owns eligibility and the frozen battle profile.

## 7. Operator tuning and GM interaction

All numbers above are launch defaults. Approved controls are named, unit-labelled fields with a current value, permitted range, explanation and example consequence. Operators use the authenticated web console to publish realm profiles through config-service; region-specific event overrides go through the owning domain's approved event workflow. An authorized in-game GM console invokes the same configuration or event workflow for its granted scope. This does not add regional cohort filters to LiveOps configuration targeting. Civic governors can change only permissions, schedules and their service tax within those operator-set limits. No console accepts executable expressions, structural solver coefficients, arbitrary SQL or client-only authority changes.

| Operator control | Launch default | Allowed publishing range | Activation rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Siege timing is selected as a complete reviewed profile, never as arbitrary independent runtime fields; each profile must provide a compatible availability window and capture duration. Claim volume ceilings and settlement requirements are authored content profiles reviewed for world and replication budgets; existing claims are grandfathered on a reduction and cannot expand until compliant. Weather/neglect rates live in Construction's bounded material/exposure controls. The mathematical charging algorithm, permission precedence, atomic consumption, safety floor, protected property, archive-before-release rule and siege protections require a versioned code/content release to change. Client-visible formulas and semantics ship with a compatible client update, with migration and advance player notice; they are never editable in-game. See Tuning and Configuration for audit, preview, authority, publication, expiry and rollback.

## 8. Persistence and integration requirements

Persist owner and district boundaries; actor/role grants and restrictions; placement provenance and condition; material/upgrade state; reserve inventory and charge receipts; entitlement and stage evidence; government decisions; notices, prepaid/grace/reclamation deadlines; siege participants, revision, objective progress and result; archived world deltas and inventory recovery receipts. Clock deadlines use authoritative elapsed real time and survive restarts. Outage recovery cannot double-debit or release land without a durable archive; ambiguous siege state pauses destructive actions until recovered.

World Simulation owns terrain persistence and safe baseline restoration. Building and Territory owns claim/structure state and authorization; Inventory owns all container movements and recovery transactions; Crafting owns recipes; Economy owns monetary taxation and ledger entries; guild governance uses existing guild authority. Construction previews consume effective permissions and config revisions; a stale preview revalidates before committing. GM spawn/build/stat tools never gain territory rights solely by being visible in-world; their capabilities use the Game Master contract.

### Registry additions

The Shared Registry registers `building-service` and the canonical claim `clm_`, structure `strc_`, siege `sge_` and construction-action `cba_` identifiers. [Construction Integration Contracts](Legends_Shattered_Frontier_Construction_Integration_Contracts_v0.1.md) defines canonical construction transaction, container/recovery, permission revision and contribution-receipt contracts. Territory supplies the authoritative land reservation, permission evaluation, maintenance charging, archive/recovery and siege behavior used by those contracts. The registry remains authoritative for exact identifiers and proposal/merge state; additions beyond its registered definitions must use its review process. There is no cross-service shared database transaction, and `building-service` never writes Inventory-owned stock or ledger rows.
