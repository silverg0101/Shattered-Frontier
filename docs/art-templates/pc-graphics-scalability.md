# Windows PC graphics scalability and asset acceptance

User-selected target, recorded 2026-10-18: **Windows PC, 2560×1440 output, 60 fps**. The frame interval is 1000/60 = **16.67 ms**. This policy supplies the missing production direction for the [model acceptance gates](model-production-gates.md); it does not accept any existing model or establish measured performance. The [development-machine record](../evidence/pc-graphics-baseline-20261018.md) is a separate, replaceable baseline candidate.

## Representation follows the view and workload

There is no permanent project-wide triangle count or texture dimension for every asset. Source triangles and atlas dimensions describe authored content; visible geometry, shader cost, resident mips, shadows, instance density, CPU work and streaming determine runtime cost. Keep useful source detail and derive representations that meet a declared scene/tier budget. Asset-specific limits remain required where they protect a measured workload; record their scope and evidence instead of generalizing them to all models.

A mature oak must read at mature-tree scale in the world. Its acceptance brief must state measured height/crown dimensions in metres, ordinary and closest camera distances, field of view, silhouette/leaf requirements and representative grove density/visible instances. Exact botanical dimensions and density remain authored decisions; do not shrink a tree or hide its canopy to manufacture a performance pass. Review one nearby tree, overlapping canopies, a grove and a distant vista with terrain, shadows and representative gameplay content. An isolated turntable cannot accept forest performance.

## Device and quality profiles

Use shipped Windows device profiles, scalability groups and player graphics settings to select compatible representations. Epic documents [device profiles and memory buckets](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/setting-up-device-profiles-in-unreal-engine); the proposed PC tiers below are project intentions, not implemented matching rules or promises about specific GPUs.

| Project tier | Purpose and permitted scaling | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compatibility | A supported lower device with reviewed lighting/reflection, shadow, material, texture and geometry alternatives; retain gameplay readability and mature-tree identity. | Choose minimum hardware/API and validate its actual fallback representations before claiming support. |
| Standard | Initial 1440p/60 validation target; balance feature groups against measured CPU/GPU and memory limits. | First candidate is this development machine; record the actual selected GPU and settings, then validate a separate lower device before setting minimum specs. |
| Enhanced | Spend proven headroom on detail, lighting/shadows or higher internal resolution while retaining the same 60 fps target. | Measure the complete representative scenes; a powerful adapter is insufficient evidence by itself. |

These project tiers are not aliases for UE Low/Medium/High/Epic. Map individual feature groups deliberately. Hardware detection supplies a conservative supported default; player changes remain within shipped capability/bounds. A controlled benchmark can refine the initial choice. If runtime adaptation is implemented, use versioned selection logic with bounded options, hysteresis and recovery; record the effective tier/settings so a capture is reproducible. Automatic adaptation is not currently implemented here.

1440p means the **output** dimensions. Record internal render dimensions/screen percentage, reconstruction method and any dynamic-resolution range separately. Include a native 100% comparison; do not label an upscaled capture as native 1440p. If lower internal resolution is selected for a tier, review foliage motion, thin leaves, shimmer and ghosting at its worst allowed value. [Epic's TSR documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine) explains why input resolution and temporal reconstruction affect quality.

## Geometry, foliage, materials and distance

- **Nanite and LOD:** evaluate Nanite for suitable detailed static geometry against conventional authored LODs, screen-space error, material sections and supported fallback/collision paths. Nanite selects detail and culls geometry, but aggregate foliage, layered surfaces and deformation still need profiling; source triangle count alone is not its acceptance test. [Working with Nanite content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content).
- **Oak foliage:** compare an appropriate leaf representation, opacity/two-sided shading, overdraw, canopy silhouette, wind/WPO bounds and shadow cost at near/middle/far views. Author branch/leaf LOD or proxy transitions where the chosen path needs them. UE 5.8's new Nanite Foliage system is explicitly **Experimental**; assemblies/voxels/skinning are an evaluation option, not the default production dependency. [Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage). The new foliage Pixel Programmable Distance option can be evaluated independently; [5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes) describe it and the Lumen Medium path.
- **Texture streaming:** author sufficient detail for intended views, retain mip chains and build/verify streaming data. Assign reviewed texture groups, compression/channel packing and per-tier mip/LOD policy. Budget the scene's resident texture pool with room for geometry, render targets, shadows, buffers and other processes; do not allocate all VRAM to textures. Record pool use, wanted/resident mips, over-budget events and travel/re-entry behavior. A universal 2K/4K cap or unlimited pool is not acceptance. [Streaming overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/texture-streaming-overview-for-unreal-engine), [streaming configuration](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/texture-streaming-configuration-in-unreal-engine). Existing surface-profile bake defaults remain prototype authoring settings.
- **World distance and HLOD:** choose measured near representation ranges, far proxies, shadow/WPO ranges and streaming/loading distances per world/tier. World Partition HLOD can render distant unloaded static content using proxies and reduce draw calls; profile proxy memory/materials and transition stability. This is a future integration requirement, not a claim that E1 uses World Partition/HLOD. [World Partition HLOD](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/world-partition---hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine). Nanite alone does not stream the gameplay world. Do not assume conventional foliage instance fade/cull settings affect Nanite: [Epic's foliage guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/foliage-mode-in-unreal-engine) documents that limitation. Validate the selected path's actual behavior. Density reduction applies only to eligible cosmetic detail; collision, resources, cover and authoritative visibility cannot depend on a player's graphics tier.
- **Lighting and depth:** choose GI/reflections, shadow method, normal/height/parallax/displacement and material complexity together. Enable a cost only for demonstrated visual benefit. UE High Lumen targets 60 fps on consoles and 5.8 adds Medium; neither statement proves 1440p/60 on this PC. [Lumen performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine). Check actual Windows RHI/SM6 and feature support against [Epic's hardware requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/hardware-and-software-specifications-for-unreal-engine) before choosing a path.

## Measurements required for production acceptance

Pin an immutable workload/profile before a performance run: exact build/cook, map/content hashes, hardware/driver/adapter, RHI, tier and all effective overrides; output/internal resolution; camera route and movement speed; tree/world dimensions, visible instance/actor/material counts; lighting/weather/wind; gameplay load; warmup and capture duration. Pin profiler configuration, VSync/frame-cap state and background workload too. Use a packaged client on the actual adapter; editor/offline/headless results are supporting evidence only.

Use the MVP's p95 client-frame convention with the updated **p95 ≤16.67 ms** target for each declared 1440p/60 workload. Report mean, p95, p99, maximum and over-budget frames/hitch durations; an average of 60 fps does not pass the p95 criterion. Formal p99/hitch tolerances, capture durations, density and headroom remain profile decisions before claiming production acceptance. A declared limit that is still missing leaves that criterion `needs-review`; it does not authorize inventing a pass. Record warm traversal and cold/startup/streaming transitions separately, including shader/PSO activity. Never discard startup hitches silently.

Capture frame, game/render/RHI thread and GPU timing, GPU pass costs, system RAM, dedicated GPU memory, texture/Nanite streaming pressure and I/O stalls. The 16.67 ms interval is a whole-frame constraint; CPU and GPU stages overlap, so adding their times is not a valid frame estimate. Use Unreal Insights and UE's performance counters to identify the bottleneck before changing content. [Epic's profiling introduction](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/introduction-to-performance-profiling-and-configuration-in-unreal-engine) describes the tools and CPU/GPU distinction. Repeat the declared route at each supported tier, verify transitions and inspect image quality alongside measurements. Device-specific pass allocations and memory reserves follow measured whole-scene costs; they are not universal per-asset triangle rules.

The engine gate records asset/category contributions and the complete scene outcome. A tree may pass appearance and fail grove shadows, streaming or render-thread cost. Narrow its supported use/profile, optimize the measured bottleneck, or retain `needs-review`; generation/export success cannot close this gate. No GPU captures or runtime acceptance were performed for this documentation change.

## Configuration ownership

Follow [Tuning and Configuration Architecture](../Game-Design/Legends_Shattered_Frontier_Tuning_and_Configuration_Architecture_v0.1.md). Profile selection/matching, algorithms, feature compatibility, fallback topology and invariant gameplay visibility ship in versioned code/content. Numeric settings are not automatically live editable. This document introduces no runtime keys or editable values.

Before implementing an adjustable tier value, declare its owner/key (or registry proposal), plain-language label/help, type, units, shipped default, safe bounds/step or allowed enum, safe fallback, scope, apply time, permission/approval, incompatible combinations, visibility and observation/rollback criteria. For example, texture-pool size needs MiB, device-specific measured default/bounds and total-memory validation; draw/WPO distance needs metres and geometry/shadow consistency checks. Unmeasured defaults/bounds remain unset and release-bound until declared. Preserve unit conversions at the engine adapter.

Player-local Graphics settings use reviewed supported options; they confer no GM/realm tuning permission. Operator-facing graphics policy/profile selection, if introduced, uses the existing shared config-service/GM/web workflow with plain-language presets and compatible-client gating. Expose no raw console expressions or arbitrary CVars as product controls. New authored representations, wider bounds and unsupported renderer changes require compatible releases. Apply changes at the declared safe boundary (immediate only when validated, otherwise reload/restart); show pending changes and allow a supported restore to the shipped preset.

## Current open production decisions

The selected output/fps target is established. Minimum/recommended PC hardware, actual baseline adapter/RHI, tier mappings/defaults, mature-oak dimensions/camera distances/forest density, resident-memory and per-pass budgets, quality floors, capture/hitch limits and world distances remain unmeasured or unauthored. They must be frozen in scoped acceptance profiles and validated before production claims. Existing model-job states and evidence are unchanged.
